CHAPTER ONE: HOW TO PLAY

Resolution. When the outcome of an action is uncertain and failure would cost
something, roll two six-sided dice and add the Aptitude the Warden names. If the
total meets or beats the Difficulty, the action succeeds. On a total of exactly
the Difficulty, the action succeeds at a cost the Warden describes. Rolling two
sixes is a Bright Success and always succeeds regardless of Difficulty.

Aptitudes. Every character has four Aptitudes: Might, Grace, Wits, and Heart.
Each is rated from 1 to 5. A newly made character distributes ten points among
the four, and no Aptitude may begin above 4. Aptitudes are not skills; the
Warden asks for the one that fits the fiction.

Difficulty. The Warden sets Difficulty before dice are rolled, never after. A
Difficulty of 7 is an ordinary task under pressure, 10 is hard, and 13 is at the
edge of what a person can do. There is no Difficulty above 15; beyond that the
answer is simply no.

Seizing. To seize a creature, make a contested roll: your Might against the
target's higher of Might or Grace. Both sides roll two dice and add the named
Aptitude. On a win the target becomes Held. A Held creature may not move away
from you and rolls at a penalty of two on every action that is not an attempt to
escape. Escaping is itself a contested roll on the escaping creature's turn.

Seizing is dangerous in the open, and a failed attempt leaves you exposed. When
a seizing attempt fails, the Warden may roll on the following table.

d6 | Mishap
1 | You lose your footing and are Shaken until your next turn
2 | Your grip tears cloth, not flesh, and the target moves freely
3 | You are dragged one pace toward whatever the target chooses
4 | Nothing further goes wrong
5 | Nothing further goes wrong
6 | You catch a limb after all; the target is Held despite the failure

Conditions. A condition lasts until something in the fiction ends it. Held is
described above. A Shaken creature rolls at a penalty of one until it takes a
turn doing nothing else. A Burning creature takes one harm at the end of each of
its turns until the fire is put out, which takes an action and a Grace roll
against Difficulty 7.

Harm. A character can take three harm before falling. Falling is not dying. A
fallen character is out of the scene and recovers after a night's rest unless
the Warden and the players agree that the fiction demands otherwise.

Boons. A character may take up to three Boons at creation. A Boon is a small
named advantage: Ember-Touched, Iron-Handed, Quiet-Footed, Long-Sighted. Some
Boons require a minimum Aptitude, which is listed with the Boon. Iron-Handed
requires Might 3 or better and lets you seize a creature one size larger than
yourself.

CHAPTER TWO: THE CINDER MARCHES

The Cinder Marches are the low country east of the Ember, where the ash falls
warm and the light never quite goes out. Three generations ago the mountain
opened and has been burning quietly ever since. The people who stayed learned to
read the ash the way sailors read weather, and they call themselves Wardens
without meaning anything grand by it. Nothing grows here that did not learn to
grow in the dark, and nothing walks here that did not learn to walk quietly.

Travellers in the Marches trade rumours the way they trade salt, and most of
what they say is at least partly true.

d6 | Rumour
1 | The mountain is not burning; something inside it is
2 | A warden's lantern will not light within sight of the old road
3 | The ash tastes of iron in the week before it snows
4 | There is a door in the Ember that opens from the far side
5 | Cinder hounds will not cross running water, and know why
6 | Someone is paying good coin for ash taken from the summit

Ashfall. Ash falls on the Marches for roughly nine days in ten, and the locals
grade it by how much of it you can see through. Thin ash is a nuisance. Heavy
ash halves the distance anyone can see and makes every Wits roll that depends on
sight one Difficulty harder. Wardens carry a length of red cord for exactly this
reason and tie themselves together in a line when the fall turns heavy.

The Old Road. The road up to the summit was cut before the mountain opened and
is the only path that does not require climbing. It is also the only place in
the Marches where nothing at all lives, which the Wardens consider a fact worth
respecting rather than a mystery worth solving.

CHAPTER THREE: BESTIARY

Cinder Hound. Might 3, Grace 4, Wits 1, Heart 1. Harm 2. A cinder hound hunts
by heat rather than by scent and is entirely uninterested in anything colder
than a living body. It attacks with a bite that leaves the target Burning on a
Bright Success.

Ash Warden. Might 2, Grace 3, Wits 4, Heart 3. Harm 3. An ash warden is a person
who has walked the Marches long enough to be counted by them. It knows the
weather, the road, and the names of everyone who has gone up the mountain in the
last forty years.
